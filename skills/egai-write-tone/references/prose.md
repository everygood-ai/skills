# Prose Mode — Descriptive Prose

Target output: product documentation, feature descriptions, guides, and other
text a general reader should find clear and easy to scan — not clipped, and
not literary. This is documentation, not literature.

## Before you draft

Reduce the source to its distinct ideas first: facts, decisions, causal
links, each named once. Drop anything that only restates, sets up, or
summarizes a point made elsewhere. Draft from that reduced set, not from a
looser first pass you plan to tighten afterward. Skipping this step is the
most common way prose output drifts back into ordinary verbose writing,
even when individual sentences look short.

## Rules

- Give one idea per sentence, roughly 10-20 words. Break apart any sentence
  that would need a semicolon, an em-dash aside, or more than one subordinate
  clause to hold its ideas — say the second idea in its own sentence instead.
- Use headings, bullet lists, numbered steps, or tables for enumerable
  content (causes, steps, options, comparisons) instead of folding it into a
  paragraph. Reserve plain paragraphs for content that is genuinely a single
  flowing argument.
- No summary paragraph that repeats the body. No hedging phrases ("it's
  worth noting," "generally speaking") unless the hedge itself is the point.
- Explain a technical term or abbreviation at its first use, then use the
  same short form consistently.
- Prefer common, plain words over rare synonyms. Avoid unexplained jargon.
- Use active voice by default. Use passive voice only when the actor is
  unknown or genuinely unimportant to the point.
- Avoid contractions unless the surrounding document already uses them
  consistently — match existing convention over imposing one.
- Use consistent terminology for the same action or concept throughout. Do
  not introduce a synonym for something already named.
- Do not use Latin abbreviations (`e.g.`, `i.e.`, `etc.`). Use gender-neutral
  terms and constructions.
- Keep code, commands, identifiers, values, and quoted text exact and
  unmodified.

## Self-check

- Could a reader unfamiliar with the topic follow this on one read? If not,
  simplify the sentence or add a missing connector, do not just shorten it.
- Does every sentence stick to one idea? Split any sentence that has a
  semicolon, an em-dash aside, or more than one subordinate clause.
- Does any sentence in the body, not only the closing paragraph, restate a
  point already made, for emphasis or transition? Cut it.
- Is any part of this a list, sequence, or comparison that would scan faster
  as a list, numbered steps, or a table than as a paragraph?
- Does the closing paragraph repeat something the body already said?
- Is the same concept always named the same way?
- Would tightening this further turn full sentences into fragments? If so,
  stop — prose still needs full sentences, just short ones.
